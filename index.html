Short-Hop — pay by distance, end to end of a line = $2.40

The rider app — try it (demo money only)

Plan a trip, tap in to get a QR ticket, tap out where you get off, and pay for the distance you rode. Open full screen · installable on a phone home screen · works offline.

Fare rule — fare = $2.40 × your distance ÷ the whole line

Distance is measured the way Washington Metrorail does: composite miles (the average of track and straight-line distance), rounded up to $0.05. The $0.80 minimum is one third of the maximum, Washington's ratio. A missed tap-out costs $2.40 because MBTA gates check entry only.

Example trips

Break-even: paying riders on a trip priced f instead of $2.40 must grow by 2.40/f − 1. The MBTA publishes no station-to-station trip table, so this is the condition a pilot must meet, not a forecast.

Explainer · narrated 3D animation on the real Copley → Medford/Tufts route

How riders use it

1 · Plan. Pick a destination; the app shows the fare and how it was calculated before you ride.

2 · Tap in, tap out. The fare is set on exit by the distance actually travelled, as on Washington's Metrorail. No tap-out = today's $2.40, so skipping the exit tap never pays.

3 · Ride further or get off early. You pay for the trip you took, never above $2.40.

4 · Two-gate check: sneaking in is no longer enough. You would have to sneak out too. Exit gates open only for a card or phone that tapped in, so someone who jumped the entry gate must pay $2.40 at the exit or evade a second time, in view of staff and cameras.

Why Boston hasn't, and how to get there

MBTA subway gates validate entry only, and most Green Line surface stops have no gates at all, so a flat fare was the only enforceable price. Contactless taps (live since Aug 2024) make an account-based tap-out possible: exit validators at gated stations first (Phase 1), then on-board validators on the Green Line.

Sources

Washington Metrorail already charges by distance with tap-in/tap-out, measured in composite miles and rounded up to $0.05 ($2.25–$6.75) (WMATA Tariff 49, effective July 1, 2026).

Method & sources

Pipeline

Ingest — official MBTA Open Data Portal files, ArcGIS feature services (paged, row-count verified) and the MBTA V3 API. Warehouse — DuckDB staging → marts with stated grain and automated data tests. Analysis — Holt-Winters / SARIMA with rolling-origin backtests, beta-binomial empirical Bayes, Kaplan-Meier survival, fixed-effects regression, and a convex inspection-allocation optimiser. Publish — this static site, refreshed weekly by CI.

Honesty rules

Every number on this page is computed from the data or cited. Methodology breaks are flagged, not blended. Associations are not called causes. Evasion figures that the MBTA does not publish are treated as scenarios.
