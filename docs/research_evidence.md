# MBTA Rider Vulnerabilities and Fare Evasion: Evidence Base

Compiled 2026-11-19. Every figure has a source URL and a note on where it came from. Labels:
- **OFFICIAL**: from the MBTA, MassDOT, the Massachusetts OIG, the FTA, the Legislature, or a peer agency's own publication.
- **PRESS**: from a news outlet reporting agency data or statements.
- **ADVOCACY**: from a think tank or advocacy group.
- **VENDOR**: from a vendor's own marketing.

Quotes are kept under 15 words. Where a figure could not be found, the report says so. No figures here are estimates of mine. Any arithmetic is marked "(derived)".

### 1.3 Enforcement policy history

- **Decriminalization:** M.G.L. c.159 §101 "eliminated the potential for criminal sanctions for fare evasion" and directed the MBTA to adopt warning and citation rules. Source: MBTA *Annual Report on MBTA Fare Evasion Warnings & Citations*, transmitted July 31, 2026. https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/SD4147.pdf (OFFICIAL)
- **Regulation 703 CMR 5.00:** Hearings were held in April 2021 and June 2022, and the regulation was adopted in 2022. Per the OIG, the Board "in 2022 finally adopted new fare collection policies (703 CMR 5.0)". 2021 hearing slides: https://d2bfbkar4atvke.cloudfront.net/sites/default/files/2021-04/2021-04-15-fare-evasion-regulations-public-hearing-slides.pdf ; 2022 text: https://d2o8eokdkim9o8.cloudfront.net/sites/default/files/2022-05/2022-05-20-703-CMR-5.00-MBTA-fare-evasion-regulations.pdf (OFFICIAL). Advocacy comments opposing license-renewal penalties: ACLU-MA https://www.aclum.org/sites/default/files/field_documents/public_comment_on_proposed_fare_evasion_regulations_703_cmr_5.00.pdf ; NCLC https://www.nclc.org/images/pdf/criminal-justice/Comments_MBTA_703_CMR_5.00.pdf
- **Zero enforcement in 2023 and 2024:** The MBTA's 2023 and 2024 annual reports show no written warnings or citations (OIG letter, footnote 3; OFFICIAL).
- **Fare Engagement Representatives:** 16 were hired and trained in October 2024 (MBTA release, Aug 21, 2025: https://www.mbta.com/news/2025-08-21/mbta-begin-fare-checks-warnings-followed-citations-be-issued-beginning-september-8). The OIG lists 19 unit staff with salaries "around $2 million", before loaded costs (OFFICIAL). Press puts the program at $2.39M–$2.4M (Streetsblog) or an operating budget of about $2.2M (WROR summarizing 7NEWS: https://wror.com/2026/10/01/mbta-spent-millions-to-get-rider-fares-paid-but-what-actually-happened/) (PRESS).
- **Early presence effect:** The MBTA said fare collection rose by up to 35% at stations where representatives were on site during the first weeks (MBTA release above; OFFICIAL). The MBTA did not separate this from ridership growth, per WROR/7NEWS (PRESS).
- **Formal enforcement from Sept 8, 2025:** First offense brings a written warning. The 1st–3rd citations are $50 and the 4th and later are $100. ID is requested, and riders who refuse may be asked to leave. Checks begin at subway gates, with buses and Green Line and Mattapan trolleys in later phases (MBTA release; OFFICIAL).
- **Results:**
  - CY2025: **88 written warnings and 0 citations** (MBTA annual report to the Legislature, July 31, 2026; OFFICIAL).
  - Through Aug 12, 2026: 117 warnings and zero fines (Streetsblog, MBTA-provided data; PRESS).
  - From Sept 2025 to fall 2026: 200 warnings and 3 citations per MassLive public records. The MBTA later said 62 more warnings brought the total to **262** (GBH, Oct 28, 2026; PRESS citing an MBTA statement).
  - GBH observed more than a dozen Green Line B/D rides and saw no rear-door boarder pay (PRESS observation).
- **Green Line focus:** "65 of the 78 stations" on the Green and Mattapan lines have no fare gates; all are street-level (OIG letter, Sept 8, 2025; OFFICIAL). An MBTA report from July 2026 reportedly found that checkers on Green Line cars improved contactless payment at street-level stops. Magnitude not published; the report was not located (Streetsblog; PRESS).

### 1.4 Gates and fare-collection hardware

- **Commuter rail gates:** North Station (2022), South Station (activated around the turn of 2025/26), Ruggles (Aug 2026), Back Bay (planned early 2027).
  - South Station ticket activations rose **33%** year over year, and on-board ticket sales rose **15%**.
  - Ruggles activations rose **44%**.
  - Gates were covered in rain/snow about **10%** of the time since February, per Keolis.
  - Source: Mass Transit/MassLive, Sept 23, 2026 (URL above; PRESS citing MBTA/Keolis).
  - Cost: gates about $1.3M to buy and about $2M to install, within a $3.5M "revenue growth" project (WBUR, Sept 8, 2026: https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/09/08/south-station-fare-gates-boston-mbta; PRESS).
  - These are activation figures, not revenue figures.
- **Subway gate height / "hardening":** I found no MBTA program to install taller or anti-jump subway gates as of Nov 2026. Gap.
- **AFC 2.0 / contactless (launched Aug 1, 2024):**
  - About 904,000 of about 10.9 million taps were contactless in August 2024, roughly 8% (derived).
  - The share passed 25% within 12 months.
  - By July 2025, about 3.17M trips involved contactless taps vs 3.26M stored-value taps.
  - Source: CommonWealth Beacon, Oct 2025: https://commonwealthbeacon.org/transportation/contactless-fares-quickly-grow-popular-among-t-riders/ (PRESS citing MBTA data).
  - On evasion, the OIG says the impact "has yet to be determined" and that rear-door readers have not stopped free boarding (OFFICIAL).

### 1.5 Affordability programs

- **Income-Eligible Reduced Fare Program** (launched Sept 2024; about 50% off; ages 18–64 enrolled in DTA, MassHealth, or MASSGrant):
  - Over 38,000 riders since launch, per the MBTA's 2025 year-end review via METRO Magazine: https://www.metro-magazine.com/news/mbta-wraps-up-2025-with-strong-improvements-across-the-system (PRESS citing MBTA).
  - "Nearly 45,000" during FY26, from a search excerpt of funding coverage. The primary document was not verified (PRESS).
  - Projection: 62,000 riders and up to 8.1M induced annual trips by 2029.
  - MBTA legislative report (Oct 2025): https://malegislature.gov/Reports/28225/(44)%20MBTA%20Income%20Eligible%20Reduced%20Fares%20_%20Legislative%20Report%20_%2010.25.pdf (OFFICIAL; not opened in full; verify figures).
- **The OIG's position:** Reduced-fare programs, "not by the failure to collect fares," are the way to address affordability (OIG letter, footnote 1).

### 1.6 Boston fare-free bus routes 23 / 28 / 29

- The program ran March 1, 2022 through June 30, 2026 and was ARPA-funded via the City (boston.gov program page: https://www.boston.gov/departments/transportation/free-route-23-28-and-29-bus-program; OFFICIAL).
- **Mid-program report (March 2023):** 1,200 survey respondents. Ridership on the free routes grew faster than the bus system overall, travel times held steady, and most riders were "transit-critical". https://boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2023/03/Fare%20Free%20Mid%20Program%20Report.pdf (OFFICIAL, City of Boston)
- **Route 28 pilot:**
  - Dwell time per passenger fell about 20% (City; OFFICIAL).
  - The City cited a 38% ridership rise.
  - The MBTA's analysis put the fare-attributable ridership gain at 22% after removing the systemwide trend.
  - Source for both: CommonWealth Beacon: https://commonwealthbeacon.org/transportation/wu-mbta-each-put-own-spin-on-fare-free-bus-results/ (PRESS reporting both agencies).
- **Gap:** No final two-year or 2026 evaluation was found.

### 1.7 Rider survey questions on evasion
I found no MBTA survey that asks about witnessing or committing evasion. The Rider Census does collect fare type and payment method (2025 full report, item list), but no evasion question was found. Gap. BART's survey-based "witnessed evasion" metric (Section 3) is a model the MBTA could copy.

## 2. Rider-facing vulnerabilities

### 2.1 2025 System-Wide Passenger Survey ("Rider Census")
Sources:
- Executive summary: https://cdn.mbta.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/2026-04-09-rider-census-report-2025-executive-summary.pdf
- Full report: https://cdn.mbta.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/2026-04-09-rider-census-report-2025-final.pdf
- Page: https://www.mbta.com/performance-metrics/the-rider-census
- Open data item: 2dac9c48bd3440c2bcf2a220614c667b on mbta-massdot.opendata.arcgis.com

All OFFICIAL.

- **Method:** A rolling annual survey of about 15,000 responses per year, on tablets in 12 languages. The 2025 release pools 2022–2025 data and is weighted by ridership and transfer rate. Known biases: mostly weekdays, 6:00AM–8:00PM.
- **It is a demographic and travel survey, not a satisfaction survey.** It does not rank complaints.
- **Key findings:**
  - 53% of riders are women.
  - 56% belong to a race/ethnicity-protected class.
  - **65% self-identify as low-income** (80% AMI threshold; 11% declined). The share is 70% on bus, 78% on Mattapan, and 50% on ferry.
  - **42% live in households with no vehicle.**
  - Among riders with an alternative way to make the trip, **taxi/rideshare is the top alternative at 43%**, up from 35% the year before and 27% in 2023. "A different MBTA service" fell from 29% to 16%.

### 2.2 Customer satisfaction
- **MBTA dashboard:** https://www.mbta.com/performance-metrics/customer-satisfaction (OFFICIAL). It uses a rider opinion panel of about 6,500 riders surveyed every four months on overall satisfaction, communication, most recent trip, and reliability, on a 7-point scale. The current values only render in the interactive dashboard and **were not retrievable**. Gap. Pull them manually or request them from opmi@mbta.com.
- **74% satisfied (somewhat, very, or extremely)** in an October MBTA rider survey, reported secondhand by the Christian Science Monitor, Nov 2025: https://www.csmonitor.com/Business/2025/1115/boston-mbta-phillip-eng (PRESS citing MBTA).
- **MassINC/Barr poll** (1,000 residents, Aug 14–20, 2023, ±3.3): https://www.massincpolling.com/our-work/poll-residents-sound-the-alarm-on-mbta-service-quality-safety (independent poll). It is older but is the best public data on drivers of dissatisfaction.
  - 70% of current and former riders felt unsafe at least once due to infrastructure condition, and 67% for other reasons.
  - Share of current riders rating service at least good: subway 36%, bus 59%, commuter rail 63%.
  - Top ways to grow ridership: free service 57%, more reliable service 54%.

### 2.3 Safety and security
- **FTA Safety Management Inspection, final report Aug 31, 2022** (fieldwork April 14 – June 30, 2022): https://www.transit.dot.gov/sites/fta.dot.gov/files/2022-08/FTA-Safety-Management-Inspection-Report-for-MBTA-and-DPU_0.pdf (OFFICIAL).
  - **20 MBTA findings in four categories:** operations/maintenance staffing (4), safety management information (6), safety communication (3), and operating conditions, procedures, and training (7).
  - Urgent Special Directives 22-4 to 22-7 came in June 2022, plus August directives to the MBTA and to DPU (22-8 and 22-13).
- **Transit Police crime:**
  - GM Eng reported a **16% reduction in recorded crime** systemwide for Jan 1 – Sept 24, 2025 vs the same period in 2024, in a letter to US DOT (Boston.com, Oct 6, 2025: https://www.boston.com/news/transportation/2025/10/06/phil-eng-responds-trump-admin-mbta-safety-demands/; PRESS quoting an OFFICIAL letter).
  - No MBTA Transit Police annual Part I table for 2025 or 2026 was retrieved. Gap.

### 2.4 Reliability and slow zones
- The Track Improvement Program (2023–2024) removed more than 220 speed restrictions and replaced about 250,000 ft of rail. Riders save 2.4M minutes per weekday. The final Green Line restrictions were lifted in Dec 2024 (METRO Magazine summarizing MBTA: https://www.metro-magazine.com/news/boston-successfully-completes-track-improvement-program; PRESS citing MBTA).
- The Orange Line reached 55 mph in 2025, per NBC Boston: https://www.nbcboston.com/news/local/mbta-2025-improvements-issues/3868842/ (PRESS).

### 3.1 Research on inspection rates and nudges
- **Optimal proof-of-payment inspection rate:** About **3.8%** of passengers in Barabino et al., *Transportation Research Part B* 70 (2014), using Italian operator data: https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:transb:v:70:y:2014:i:c:p:1-17. A later model gives **3.4%–4.0%** (*Networks and Spatial Economics*, 2019): https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11067-019-09468-3. Both rest on one operator's data. Compare MBTA staffing of 16 representatives against more than half a million weekday riders.
- **TCRP Synthesis 96 / Report 80:** Off-board POP practice, covering inspection rates, adjudication, and penalties. https://www.trb.org/main/blurbs/166757.aspx
- **Enforcement design:** Dai, Galeotti and Villeval found that random controls beat concentrated crackdowns (Bonn CRC TR 224 working paper: https://www.wiwi.uni-bonn.de/bgsepapers/boncrc/CRCTR224_2023_451v2.pdf).
- **Behavioral nudges:** Ayal, Celse and Hochman (2021, *OBHDP* 166) ran a French rail-station field experiment. Eye-cue posters alone did nothing. Combined with a descriptive social-norm message, they reduced evasion detected in standard inspections. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jobhdp:v:166:y:2021:i:c:p:9-19. Effect size not extracted. A survey experiment (Celse and Grolleau, *Transport Policy* 2023) found that beliefs about how common evasion is drive the behavior: https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:trapol:v:138:y:2023:i:c:p:119-128
- **Fare capping / free fares:** Boston's Route 28 is the local evidence (Section 1.6). No measured evasion effect of fare capping was found. Gap.

## 4. Technology approaches

- **Computer-vision gate evasion detection:**
  - Cubic FEnX gate (Feb 2025) claims to detect and classify pushing, climbing, and tailgating without capturing PII. https://www.cubic.com/news-events/news/cubic-announces-the-fenx-fare-gate (VENDOR; no independent results).
  - Barcelona FGC uses the AWAAIT camera system to alert inspectors at barriers and reports reduced evasion, with no figure given. https://www.axis.com/en-us/customer-story/reducing-fare-evasion-public-transport (VENDOR).
- **NYC "smart" gates:** Wide-aisle, taller double-door gates with sensors from the 35% pilot above (OFFICIAL outcome). See also Planetizen, Dec 2025: https://www.planetizen.com/news/2025/12/136588-new-york-mta-installs-smart-fare-gates-stop-turnstile-hoppers (PRESS).
- **AFC/APC data matching:** This is how the MassDOT bus study measured non-interaction. It is the cheapest measurement tool the MBTA already owns and is directly reproducible with MBTA open data.
- **Platform screen doors:** No MBTA-relevant measured evasion or safety evidence was gathered. Gap.

## 6. Open gaps

1. **No current official MBTA evasion rate by mode.** The MBTA publishes "fare engagement" ratios and enforcement counts only. Ask OPMI for the engagement time series by line and station.
2. **No current official revenue-loss estimate.** The newest official figures are 2019 (bus $6.0M–$7.4M; commuter rail $10M–$20M). The widely repeated "$25–30M" traces to advocacy and opinion pieces.
3. **The July 2026 MBTA report on Green Line fare checkers** was not located.
4. **Customer satisfaction values** from the MBTA dashboard (6,500-rider panel) were not retrievable. Download them manually. The MBTA has no published ranked "top complaints" list. Customer-complaint (feedback) data was not found on the open data portal.
5. **The Rider Census has no evasion or satisfaction questions.** Its open-data item 2dac9c48bd3440c2bcf2a220614c667b should be checked directly for fare-payment-method fields.
6. **Transit Police Part I crime tables for 2025–2026** were not retrieved. Use MBTA Transit Police annual reports or FBI CDE.
7. **Official elevator and escalator availability %** was not found.
8. **No final evaluation of the fare-free 23/28/29 program** (it ended June 30, 2026).
9. **Income-eligible reduced fare enrollment:** the FY26 "nearly 45,000" figure is unverified. Open the Oct 2025 legislative report.
10. **Peer evidence caveats:** WMATA's 82% has no published method. BART's metric is survey-based. LA Metro has no gate-pilot evaluation. Paris year-end 2025 outcomes were not found. Platform screen doors and computer vision have no independent measured outcomes (vendor claims only).
11. **The MassDOT bus study's "non-interaction"** includes legitimately free riders, so it should not be called an "evasion rate" without that caveat.
